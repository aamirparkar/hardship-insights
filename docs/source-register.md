## S01: The Invisible Dropout

Vlad Cvetkovski, Humbli, April 2026; March 2026 website scan. [Publisher page](https://www.humbli-insights.com/post/humbli-whitepaper-the-invisible-dropout-findings-from-the-financial-hardship-accessibility-index) and [PDF](https://8b0c723c-d7e1-42d6-858a-227391935846.usrfiles.com/ugd/8b0c72_051a192bd37c4e2b8a13f210f0a4c892.pdf).

Relevant sections: 3, 4.6 and 6 (PDF pages 8-9, 12 and 16). The study covers public website content across 120 lenders; it excludes internal process quality and customer outcomes. This supports keeping access, operational and outcome measures distinct.

Project interpretation: a website exit does not establish financial hardship or unmet need. Do not reproduce the HAI, infer its weights or calibrate synthetic outcomes to published sector figures.

## S02: Early Engagement in Hardship

Brendan Maggs, Humbli, August 2025. [Publisher page](https://www.humbli-insights.com/post/humbli-whitepaper-early-engagement-in-hardship-lifting-capability-across-the-sector) and [PDF](https://8b0c723c-d7e1-42d6-858a-227391935846.usrfiles.com/ugd/8b0c72_e49db92bb18b486aa21dd70c654d430f.pdf).

Relevant sections: barriers, empowerment and delivery (PDF pages 11-14). The paper discusses trust, awareness, psychological safety and practical knowledge as influences on engagement, and describes interactive learning within digital journeys.

Project interpretation: demonstrate descriptive early/later contact comparisons, with selection limitations. The paper's case study does not establish an intervention effect for this project. Simulator probabilities are authored assumptions.

## S03: Customer Empowerment: Self-efficacy and the power of the moment

Brendan Maggs, Humbli, October 2024. Local source: `White Paper 1.pdf` (local reference copy, not distributed), 20 PDF pages, 6,998,595 bytes. SHA-256: `ef52cea73b0540c5a095e407e81423e9339b840fba54797907f7e12ebe154ed5`.

All pages were processed using embedded font-to-Unicode mappings. Pages 5, 7, 10 and 13 yielded no text; graphical content remains unreviewed. Key sections: customer education (pages 6, 8-9), model (11), InsightBridging (12), case studies (14-15), future work (16), references (18).

The paper emphasises confidence in decision-making, timely learning, accessibility and continuous refinement. It describes modular learning and xAPI analytics. Its 2022 survey involved 1,696 prospective homebuyers and guarantors; this is not a hardship customer sample. Electricity and broker examples are contextual case studies, not controlled evidence of hardship outcomes. Intent to act, registrations and self-reported confidence do not establish subsequent behaviour or causal impact.

Project decision: separate exposure, completion, knowledge responses, confidence and action. Do not copy branded learning designs or claim to implement the proprietary model. Underlying references, including Bandura and the learning literature, have not been independently reviewed.

## S04: Humbli product positioning

[Insights and Intelligence](https://www.humbli-insights.com/insights-and-intelligence), inspected 4 November 2026. The publisher describes standardised contributions, time trends, anonymised peer comparisons and macroeconomic context. It distinguishes accessibility (HAI) from support after a request (HSI). These are publisher product descriptions, not independent validation or a public specification of either index.

Project decision: use our own transparent metric contracts. Include fictional institution trends and data coverage; defer peer rankings until populations and definitions are comparable. Do not call this project HAI, HSI or a reproduction of the Financial Hardship Support Standard.

## S05: Dynamic Disclosure product announcement

[Publisher announcement](https://www.humbli-insights.com/post/enough-is-enough-aussie-start-up-launches-customer-first-product-disclosure-platform), body dated June 2025; inspected 4 November 2026. It describes deliberate reflection points, embedded learning and interaction tracking. This is a marketing account of a disclosure product, not measured evidence that added friction improves hardship support.

Project decision: distinguish unnecessary administrative friction from purposeful learning. Faster completion and fewer exits are not sufficient evidence of understanding. Keep disclosure-product replication outside the first release.

## S06: ASIC REP 782

[Report](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/w35baapb/rep782-published-20-may-2024-20240809.pdf), May 2024, inspected relevant executive-summary and findings text directly. Paragraphs 15-18 and Table 1 discuss customer care, process barriers, communications and oversight. The approximately 35% withdrawal/non-response finding concerns customers after a hardship notice; it is not a website abandonment rate. Review of processes at 10 large home lenders and broader data collection must not be treated as the same sample or as all Australian lenders.

Project decision: retain closure reason and status; expose pending cases and process handoffs. Our initial one-request-per-customer design cannot reproduce a customer-level measure of dropping out at least once across repeat requests. No direct benchmark comparison is valid.

## S07: ASIC REP 815

[Report](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/db0d1jmf/rep-815-hardship-not-as-hard-to-get-help-published-25-september-2025.pdf), 25 September 2025; relevant overview and findings on PDF pages 3-8 inspected. It follows REP 782 and reports improvements alongside continuing concerns. Follow-up observations cover January 2024-June 2025; identification and recording changes affect interpretation of notice volumes. It discusses process flexibility and communication around assistance expiry.

Project decision: version definitions and capture changes. Higher notice volume may reflect improved identification or access as well as greater difficulty. Include end-of-assistance contact and distinguish trends from attribution. This is a later source than REP 782, not a claim that no newer publications exist.
